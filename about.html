About the research

As part of the MDP Heliophysics research project, the software team is improving the quality and accessibility of spectrograms from the Ground Radio Lab and Long Wavelength Array antennas. The long-term goal is to help researchers and students detect and classify solar radio bursts.

Solar radio bursts are high-intensity radio emissions from the Sun. Their patterns in a spectrogram can help distinguish different burst types.

Software team

Maria Hermann, Zoe Fisch, Ariv Bhatnagar, Divya Shekar, Ella Ricci, Praneel Basu, and Erica Ma.

The team is building a public platform for viewing spectrograms and, eventually, running the preprocessing, detection, and classification models. A key goal is to make observations accessible to students at Marquette High School.

Data and model workflow

Training data comes from public datasets and the team's observations at Marquette and Peach Mountain. At Marquette, an LWA antenna, e-Callisto receiver, and CALLISTO software produce FIT files for daily upload.

The detection approach uses YOLOv8 to locate candidate bursts with bounding boxes in spectrogram tiles. A separate classification model for solar radio burst types is planned.

Project goals and next steps

  • Improve detection accuracy and implement burst classification.
  • Grow the labeled dataset using Marquette and Peach Mountain observations.
  • Connect this website to the models and make spectrogram visualization and results easier to access.

This site is currently a front-end demo. The spectrogram and detection on the Detector page are synthetic examples; uploaded files are not analyzed until a backend is connected.
